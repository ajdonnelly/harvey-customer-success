About

I think like a mid-market Harvey client

Because I come from that world — I’ve been on the customer side of these decisions.

Most recently, as a senior litigation paralegal, I helped design and support my firm’s enterprise legal-AI pilot. I’ve served on the AI committee, helped run sessions and office hours, worked with tools like Microsoft Copilot and Filevine LOIS, and dealt directly with the challenge of getting everyone from staff to partners comfortable enough with a new tool to actually use it. I understand what it takes to move a firm from simply having access to building real habits around the technology.

Before that, I spent more than a decade at a small law firm where I was involved in much more than litigation work. I dealt with clients, billing and receivables, and the practical decisions around what technology we paid for, kept, replaced, or stopped using. So managing a book of business, watching customer health, and having conversations about value and spend all make intuitive sense to me.

I’m making the move into customer success deliberately. What I want to do is help law firms actually get lasting value out of legal AI — not just buy it.

How I’d run a mid-market book

Consultative where it matters, standardized where it can be

With 40-plus firms, you can’t treat every account like a bespoke engagement. I’d want to be a prescriptive advisor on the things that actually drive adoption and value, while turning the repeatable parts into playbooks, regular cadences, training, and self-service resources that can work across the whole book.

The goal is a simple loop you can run consistently across every account: understand where they are, identify what is blocking adoption, intervene where it matters, and then take what works and scale it.

01

Drive adoption

Prove it’s safe, then get every account to a first win fast — and on to a daily habit, with guided onboarding, office hours, and use-case templates that scale across the book.

02

Evidence the value

Turn usage into documented ROI — time saved, workflows touched — and bring it to a crisp business review the sponsor can defend internally.

03

Watch health, act early

Monitor adoption and sentiment across the book; catch churn signals weeks early and intervene with a targeted play, not a renewal-day scramble.

04

Expand on proof

When value is real, grow it — more seats, a second practice group, a new workflow — so net revenue retention climbs past 100%.

Why Harvey, and why this role

The product I’d be proud to make customers successful with

Harvey is defining a category, and mid-market customer success sits right at the intersection of adoption, value, and revenue. That’s exactly where I want to work.

I’d bring legal credibility that customers can feel pretty quickly, a strong understanding of what actually drives adoption inside a law firm, and the discipline to manage a large book without treating every account like a one-off project. Because I know these workflows from the inside, I can be a practical, prescriptive advisor to customers and also bring useful frontline feedback back to Product and Legal Engineering.

There is an obvious gap in my background, and I’d rather just say it directly: I haven’t yet owned a formal renewals quota or managed a book of accounts in a CS platform. What I do have is the part that I think is harder to teach — deep legal-domain knowledge and firsthand experience getting people to actually adopt new technology. I can learn the commercial mechanics quickly. The customer empathy, judgment, and understanding of how firms really work are already there.

And Harvey’s values make sense to me: make decisions, keep things simple, and keep working until the job is actually done.
