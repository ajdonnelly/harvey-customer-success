# Harvey: Your First Win — Articulate AI Build Script

**What this is.** A ready-to-use prompt and content spec for building a short, self-paced onboarding module in **Articulate Rise (AI Assistant)**. Paste the *Master Prompt* below into Rise's AI to scaffold the course, then use the *Lesson-by-Lesson Spec* to fill and refine each lesson.

**The premise of the module (its whole spine).** New legal users hesitate for two reasons: they're not sure it's *safe*, and they're not sure where to *start*. This module removes both blocks in one short sitting — first it shows Harvey is safe and how it actually produces its answers, then it walks the user to a **confident, quick win**: extracting structured data from a set of documents in **Vault**. Prove it's safe → reach a first win → build the habit.

**Audience.** Attorneys, paralegals, and legal staff who are brand-new to Harvey. No technical background assumed.

**Tone.** Clear, warm, plain-language, and encouraging. Short sentences. No hype. Respect the reader's expertise as a legal professional while assuming zero AI knowledge.

**Length / format.** ~15–20 minutes. Five short lessons plus a close. Use Rise blocks: text, statement/quote, image or screenshot placeholders, accordion or process/timeline for steps, and a Knowledge Check at the end of each lesson with supportive feedback.

## Lesson-by-Lesson Spec

### Lesson 1 — Harvey is safe: the trust foundation

**Objective:** The learner feels confident putting real, confidential matter documents into Harvey (within their firm's own policies).

**On-screen content:**
- **Enterprise-grade security.** Your data is encrypted both in transit and at rest.
- **Your data isn't used to train AI models.** By default, Harvey does not use customer data to train AI models — regardless of which underlying model powers a given answer — unless you explicitly authorize it.
- **Independently audited.** Harvey maintains **SOC 2 Type II** and **ISO 27001** certifications, renewed through regular third-party audits.
- **You stay in control.** You choose what to upload, set your own retention policies, delete data at any time, and can keep data in-region.
- **What this means for you:** you can bring real matter documents into Harvey and do real work — inside your firm's approved policies and matter-intake rules.

**Statement block (pull-quote):** "Harvey is built so you can use it on the work that matters — not just on practice files."

**Knowledge Check (true/false):**
- *"Harvey uses the documents I upload to train its AI models."* → **False.** By default your data is never used to train models unless you explicitly authorize it.

### Lesson 2 — How Harvey knows what it knows: grounding and citations

**Objective:** The learner understands Harvey answers from *sources*, not guesswork — and that their job is to verify.

**On-screen content:**
- **Harvey grounds its answers in real sources — starting with yours.** It works from the material you give it: your uploaded files, your Vault projects, and connected document systems — plus vetted legal sources (case law, statutes, regulations, and secondary authorities across a large library of legal databases and curated public sources).
- **It's not a chatbot guessing from the open web.** Retrieval is jurisdiction-aware and grounded in authoritative material, so results are narrower, more relevant, and easier to check.
- **Every answer is cited.** Harvey ties its response back to the exact source it used, so you can click through and confirm.
- **Your move: verify against source.** Harvey accelerates the work; the lawyer still owns the output. Check the citation before you rely on it — this is the single habit that makes AI safe to use on real matters.

**Process block (3 steps): You give it sources → Harvey retrieves and reasons over them → It answers *with citations you can check.***

**Knowledge Check (multiple choice):**
- *"Where does Harvey get the information in its answers?"*
  - A) It makes it up from general knowledge
  - B) From a generic open-web search
  - **C) From the sources you give it and vetted legal databases — and it cites them** ✓
  - D) It can't show you where an answer came from

### Lesson 3 — Talking to Harvey: a simple way to prompt

**Objective:** The learner has one repeatable structure for asking, so they don't freeze at the blank box.

**On-screen content:**
- A good request has three parts:
  1. **Intent** — what you actually want ("I need the key dates from these leases").
  2. **Context** — the background and the documents to use ("use the files in this Vault project; these are commercial leases").
  3. **Instruction** — the specific task and the format you want back ("list each lease, its commencement date, expiration date, and renewal option, as a table").
- **Point Harvey at the right source** — a specific Vault project or set of files — so it works from the right material.
- **Ask for citations,** then **iterate:** refine, narrow, or add a column until the output is what you need.

**Two-column compare (weak vs. strong prompt):**
- *Weak:* "Tell me about these leases."
- *Strong:* "From the leases in this project, build a table with tenant, commencement date, expiration date, and renewal option. Quote the renewal language verbatim and cite the page."

**Knowledge Check (pick the stronger prompt):** present the two prompts above; correct answer is the strong one, with feedback naming the three parts it includes.

### Lesson 4 — Choosing your first workflow: what makes a great first task

**Objective:** The learner can spot a first use case that will succeed — building trust before tackling open-ended judgment work.

**On-screen content — a great first workflow is:**
- **High-frequency** — something you do often, so the payoff repeats.
- **Clear before/after** — the time saved is obvious.
- **Structured and checkable** — the output is easy to verify against the source.
- **Bounded** — a defined set of documents, not an open-ended question.
- **Low-risk** — not a bet-the-matter filing on day one.

**Coaching line:** "Start where the payoff is obvious and the answer is easy to check. Win once, and you'll trust it with harder work."

**Knowledge Check (multiple choice — best first workflow):**
- A) Draft a novel argument on an unsettled point of law
- **B) Pull key terms from a folder of 40 leases into a table** ✓
- C) Predict the outcome of a pending motion
- D) Summarize your firm's entire matter history
- *Feedback:* B is high-frequency, bounded, structured, and easy to verify — the profile of a great first win.

### Lesson 5 — Your first win: extract structured data from documents with Vault

**Objective:** The learner completes (or clearly sees how to complete) one concrete extraction and verifies it.

**Framing:** "This is the workflow new users win with most. It hits every criterion from Lesson 4 — and it's exactly the kind of task that used to eat an afternoon."

**What Vault is (short):** Vault is Harvey's workspace for large-scale document review and analysis. You can organize, review, and query large sets of documents — up to thousands per project — and query them directly in Assistant.

**Step-by-step (use a process/timeline block):**
1. **Create a project and add your files.** Make a new Vault project and upload your document set — drag-and-drop or bulk upload, organized into folders. Vault handles everything from a handful of PDFs to large data rooms.
2. **Extract the data — two paths.**
   - **One-click workflow:** for common document types (e.g., merger agreements, stock purchase agreements, leases, limited partnership agreements, court opinions), a single click extracts 25+ data points per document. These were engineered by Harvey's team of former BigLaw attorneys and AI researchers and evaluated for high recall against attorney-verified ground truth.
   - **Custom review table:** build your own — add one column per data point you want, and choose each column's data type (date, currency, verbatim text, and more). Harvey even suggests a type. The **Verbatim** type pulls the exact clause word-for-word — useful when you need the language, not a paraphrase.
3. **Review and verify.** Read the table; sort and filter; click any cell to open the source and confirm it against the document. Add or remove columns to refine. This is the verify-against-source habit from Lesson 2, in practice.
4. **Reuse it.** Save the workflow and share custom extraction workflows with your team, so your firm's expertise is built into the tool and everyone benefits.

**Statement block:** "What took an afternoon of skimming and copying now takes minutes — with a citation behind every value."

**Knowledge Check (two items):**
- *Match the column type:* "You need the exact wording of each renewal clause." → **Verbatim.**
- *Before you rely on an extracted value, you should…* → **open the cited source and confirm it against the document.**

### Lesson 6 — Close: the job's not finished

**On-screen content:**
- **Your 30-minute first task:** pick a bounded set — a folder of NDAs, leases, or a single deal's agreements — create a Vault project, run a one-click workflow or build a five-column review table, and verify five rows against source. That's your first win.
- **Then build the habit:** each time you reach for a manual, repetitive document task, ask whether it's a Vault extraction. The wins compound.
- **Close line:** "You proved it's safe. You got a win. Now keep going — the job's not finished."

**Final CTA button:** "Start your first Vault project."
